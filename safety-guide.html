Safety & Emergency Guide 🚨

Bad trip de-escalation, crisis intervention, and knowing when to seek medical help. Harm reduction saves lives.

Emergency Crisis Line
911
If someone is having a medical emergency - seizures, unconsciousness, chest pain, extreme hyperthermia, violent behavior - call 911 immediately. Paramedics are trained for this and will not involve law enforcement for drug use alone in most jurisdictions.

Bad Trip De-Escalation Protocol

First, understand: There's no such thing as a "bad trip" - only difficult experiences. Most "bad trips" are panic attacks or challenging moments that can be navigated with proper support.

🎯 The Sitter's Role: CALM Protocol

If you're helping someone having a difficult time, follow C.A.L.M.:

  1. Connect - Make eye contact, speak gently, be present. "I'm here with you. You're safe."
  2. Anchor - Ground them in reality. "You took mushrooms. This is temporary. You will return."
  3. Listen - Let them express fears without judgment. Validate their experience.
  4. Move - Change environment if needed. Different room, music, lighting. Small changes help.

Your calm presence is the medicine. Stay centered.

💬 What to Say (Sitter Scripts)

When they're panicking:

  • "You're safe. I'm here with you."
  • "You took [substance]. This is temporary. You will return to normal."
  • "Thousands of people have felt exactly what you're feeling right now, and they're all okay."
  • "Can you breathe with me? In for 4, out for 6."

When they think they're dying:

  • "Your ego is dissolving, not your body. This is what ego death feels like."
  • "You're not dying - you're experiencing a profound state of consciousness. It will pass."
  • "I'm watching you. Your breathing is fine. Your heart is fine. You're safe."

When they think it will never end:

  • "Look at me. This will end. Usually in [X] more hours. You will be completely back to normal."
  • "Your brain is working differently right now, so time feels weird. But it's passing normally."
  • "I've seen this before. Everyone returns. You will too."
✓ DO THIS
  • Stay calm yourself
  • Maintain gentle eye contact
  • Speak slowly and clearly
  • Use their name
  • Guide breathing exercises
  • Change music/lighting
  • Offer water
  • Physical comfort if wanted
  • Remind them it's temporary
  • Validate their experience
✗ DON'T DO THIS
  • Panic or show fear
  • Tell them to "calm down"
  • Argue with their reality
  • Touch them without permission
  • Turn on bright lights suddenly
  • Call police unless medical emergency
  • Leave them alone
  • Give them more substances
  • Restrain them (unless danger)
  • Mock or minimize their fear

Physical Grounding Techniques

Physical anchors help bring someone back to their body:

🤝 Hand Holding

Ask permission first: "Can I hold your hand?"

Hold firmly but gently. The physical connection reminds them they're not alone and anchors them in reality. Some people report this single action saved their experience.

🫂 Grounding Touch

With permission, place hand on their shoulder or back. Firm, steady pressure. Say: "Feel my hand. This is real. You are here. You are safe."

🧊 Temperature Change

Cold water on face or wrists. Hold ice cubes. Splash cold water. Temperature shock can interrupt panic spirals and bring attention back to body.

👃 Smell Something Strong

Peppermint oil, citrus peel, strong tea. Engaging olfactory sense grounds people in present moment.

When to Call 911

Call 911 immediately if you observe:

⚠️ Medical Emergencies
Cardiovascular:
- Chest pain or pressure
- Irregular heartbeat that doesn't resolve
- Severe breathing difficulty (not just hyperventilation)

Neurological:
- Seizures
- Loss of consciousness
- Unresponsiveness to stimuli
- Severe confusion that doesn't improve

Physical:
- Extreme hyperthermia (very high fever + muscle rigidity)
- Uncontrollable vomiting/diarrhea leading to dehydration
- Signs of stroke (facial drooping, slurred speech, weakness)

Behavioral:
- Violent behavior toward self or others
- Credible suicide threats
- Psychotic break that persists (rare with mushrooms, possible with higher doses)

When in doubt, call. Better safe than sorry.
💡 What to Tell Paramedics
Be honest. Tell them:
- What substance was taken
- How much (if known)
- When it was taken
- Any other substances/medications
- Medical history if known

They need this information to help. They deal with this regularly and won't judge. In most jurisdictions, calling for medical help does NOT result in criminal charges for possession/use.

Dangerous Drug Interactions

NEVER mix psilocybin with:

⚠️ Critical Interactions
SSRIs/SNRIs (antidepressants):
- Significantly reduce psilocybin effects (waste of mushrooms)
- Possible serotonin syndrome at high doses
- Taper off for 2-4 weeks before tripping if safe to do so (with doctor approval)

MAOIs:
- DANGEROUS interaction
- Can cause serotonin syndrome
- Never combine

Lithium:
- VERY DANGEROUS
- Can cause seizures
- Never mix under any circumstances

Tramadol:
- Risk of serotonin syndrome
- Avoid combination

Heavy alcohol:
- Increases nausea
- Dulls the experience
- Save the drinking for another day

Always check drug interactions if you're on any medications.

Recognizing Serotonin Syndrome

Rare but serious condition from too much serotonin. Usually from mixing substances. Call 911 if suspected.

Symptoms (usually multiple present):

  • Agitation or restlessness
  • Confusion or disorientation
  • Rapid heart rate and high blood pressure
  • Dilated pupils
  • Loss of muscle coordination or twitching
  • Muscle rigidity
  • Heavy sweating
  • Diarrhea
  • Fever (high temperature)
  • Seizures
🚨 This is a Medical Emergency
Serotonin syndrome can be life-threatening. If you suspect it, call 911 immediately. Tell them about all substances taken. This is most likely to happen when mixing psychedelics with SSRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic drugs.
