Navigating Your Trip 🧭

What to expect during your journey, how to navigate challenges, and techniques for staying grounded when reality bends.

The Golden Rule: Whatever happens, it's temporary. You took a substance, it will wear off, you will return. This knowledge is your anchor when things get intense. Remember it, trust it, surrender to it.
"Let Go or Be Dragged"
Ancient wisdom for psychedelic journeys. Resistance creates suffering. Surrender creates flow.
⏰

Timeline: What to Expect

Every trip has predictable phases. Knowing what's coming reduces anxiety.

Phase 1: Onset
0-45 minutes

What's happening: The substance is beginning to take effect. Your body is processing it.

Physical sensations:

  • Stomach butterflies or mild nausea (normal)
  • Body feeling lighter or heavier
  • Temperature fluctuations
  • Yawning, restlessness, or nervous energy

Mental state: Anticipation, maybe some anxiety. Thoughts might start moving faster. Colors may seem brighter.

What to do: Stay calm, breathe, trust your preparation. Play your music. Get comfortable. This is just the beginning - don't judge the whole trip by the come-up.

Phase 2: Come Up
45min - 1.5 hours

What's happening: Effects are intensifying rapidly. This is often the most challenging phase.

What you might experience:

  • Visual distortions starting - patterns, breathing walls
  • Time dilation (minutes feel like hours)
  • Anxiety or "oh shit, this is really happening" feeling
  • Difficulty focusing or following conversations
  • Laughter or emotional waves

Common thought: "Did I take too much?" (Probably not - this is normal intensity building)

What to do: SURRENDER. Don't fight it. Lie down, close your eyes, breathe. Let the music carry you. If you resist, you'll have a harder time. This phase passes - you're heading to the peak.

Phase 3: Peak
1.5 - 4 hours

What's happening: You're at maximum effect. Reality is fully transformed.

Common experiences:

  • Intense visuals - geometric patterns, fractals, entities (if eyes closed)
  • Ego dissolution - boundaries between self and universe blur
  • Profound insights about life, death, existence
  • Deep emotional releases - crying, laughing, awe
  • Time stops having meaning
  • Feeling of unity with everything

What to do: BE. Don't try to control or direct the experience. Observe. Experience. Trust. This is why you're here. Surrender completely. Let the mushrooms/substance show you what they want to show you.

Note: You likely won't want to do anything during this phase. Don't force yourself to. Just experience.

Phase 4: Plateau & Come Down
4 - 6 hours

What's happening: Effects are gradually decreasing. You're returning.

What you might notice:

  • Visuals fading but still present
  • Ability to think more clearly returning
  • Insights still flowing but more grounded
  • Emotional afterglow - peace, gratitude, love
  • Ability to communicate improving

What to do: Good time to journal (voice memos if writing is hard). Talk with your sitter. Process what you experienced. Hydrate. Eat something light if you're hungry. Reflect gently.

Phase 5: Return
6 - 8+ hours

What's happening: You're mostly back to baseline. Some residual effects may linger.

Common feelings:

  • Exhaustion but difficulty sleeping
  • Afterglow - everything feels profound and beautiful
  • Desire to integrate and make sense of it all
  • Gratitude or emotional sensitivity

What to do: Rest. Don't fight sleep if it comes. Journal more. Avoid overstimulation (no intense TV, news, social media). Process gently. Take care of your body - water, light food, comfort.

Navigating Challenging Moments

Difficult moments happen. They're not "bad trips" - they're challenging parts of the journey. Here's how to navigate them:

🌊 The WAVE Technique

When anxiety or fear arises, remember: W.A.V.E.

  1. Welcome the feeling - Don't fight it. Say "okay, I see you"
  2. Accept it's temporary - This will pass, it always does
  3. Visualize it as a wave - It rises, peaks, and falls away
  4. Exhale deeply - Long, slow breaths anchor you

Resistance = Suffering. Acceptance = Flow.

🧘 Breath Anchoring

When you feel ungrounded or panicky:

  1. Place one hand on your chest, one on your belly
  2. Breathe in slowly for 4 counts
  3. Hold for 4 counts
  4. Exhale for 6 counts
  5. Repeat until you feel more centered

Your breath is always with you. It's your lifeline back to safety.

🎵 Music Change

Music profoundly affects your journey. If things feel dark or heavy:

  • Switch to something lighter, more uplifting
  • Nature sounds (rain, ocean, forest)
  • Familiar music you love and feel safe with
  • Ask your sitter to change it if you can't

Music guides the journey. Changing the soundtrack changes the experience.

👁️ Eyes Open/Closed Switching

Toggle between internal and external focus:

  • Eyes closed: Deep introspection, intense visuals, inner work
  • Eyes open: Grounding in physical reality, less intensity

If internal space feels too intense, open your eyes and look at something calming. If external world is overwhelming, close your eyes and go inward.

💬 Talk to Your Sitter

Your sitter is there for this moment:

  • Tell them you're feeling scared/overwhelmed
  • Ask them to remind you that you're safe and this is temporary
  • Request physical grounding - hand holding, hug
  • Have them guide you through breathing

Don't suffer in silence. That's literally why the sitter is there.

✅

What's Normal vs What's Concerning

These are NORMAL and not dangerous:

  • Feeling like you're dying or losing your mind (ego death - uncomfortable but safe)
  • Intense emotions - crying, laughing, fear, awe
  • Nausea or stomach discomfort (especially first hour)
  • Feeling cold or hot (temperature regulation gets weird)
  • Inability to speak coherently
  • Complete loss of time sense
  • Feeling like the trip will never end (it will)
  • Thinking you've broken your brain (you haven't)
⚠️ When to Get Help
Seek medical help if:

- Chest pain or difficulty breathing (not just rapid breathing)
- Seizures
- Loss of consciousness
- Extreme hyperthermia (very high fever, not just feeling warm)
- Violent behavior toward self or others
- Signs of serotonin syndrome (if mixed with SSRIs: confusion, rapid heart rate, high blood pressure, dilated pupils, muscle rigidity)

Call 911 if genuinely concerned. Paramedics deal with this regularly and won't judge.

Understanding Ego Death

What is ego death? The temporary dissolution of your sense of self. You forget who you are, where you are, that you took a substance. "You" disappear.

What it feels like:

  • Complete loss of identity and boundaries
  • Feeling of dying or ceasing to exist
  • Merging with the universe/everything
  • No distinction between self and other
  • Terror or bliss (or both)

Important truths:

  • You are not actually dying. Your ego is temporarily dissolving.
  • This is considered a peak mystical experience
  • It's temporary - your ego reassembles as you come down
  • Many report it as the most meaningful experience of their life
  • Resisting makes it terrifying. Surrendering makes it transcendent.
💡 If Ego Death Happens
Surrender completely. You cannot fight this and win. Let go. Trust. The universe is not trying to kill you - it's showing you that you are more than your ego. This is the gift. Breathe and let it happen. You will return.
🔄

Breaking Thought Loops

What are thought loops? Getting stuck on the same thought/realization over and over. "Oh wow, I just realized..." then forgetting, then realizing again, repeat infinitely.

How to break them:

  • Change your environment - Move to a different room
  • Change the music - New stimulus breaks the loop
  • Physical action - Stand up, stretch, walk
  • Engage senses differently - Drink water, feel a texture, smell something
  • Ask sitter for help - External voice can interrupt the loop

Thought loops are harmless but uncomfortable. They always end. Don't panic - just change something in your environment.
